Demo mode: not running on Windows, so devices and apps are simulated tone generators.

Output

The mix goes to the playback side of a virtual audio cable (for VB-CABLE, CABLE Input). Other apps then choose the cable's recording side (CABLE Output) as their microphone, and hear the whole mix.

Don't send the mix to your speakers or headphones. To hear it yourself, use Listen on Master.

Mixer

  • Each card is a source, mixed into Master, which goes to the output.
  • Drag a volume slider, or click its dB value to type one. Double-click a slider to reset it to 0 dB.
  • Drag a card by its handle, or focus the handle and use the arrow keys, to reorder.
  • Microphone cards have a ⚙ for noise suppression and the noise gate.
  • Listen on Master plays the final mix on your Windows default output, so you can check what others hear (with about 100 ms delay).

Soundboard

  • Click a sound to play it into the mix. Clicking it again restarts it.
  • Add MP3, WAV, OGG, FLAC or M4A files up to 60 seconds long with Add sounds, or drop them on the soundboard.
  • A sound's ⚙ renames it, sets its volume, trims it or deletes it. Drag sounds to reorder them, or focus one and press Alt+← or Alt+→.
  • Hear sounds also plays them on your Windows default output at Your volume, so you hear them without hearing your own voice.

Music

  • Plays audio files and whole folders into the mix, one track after another. Files stay where they are.
  • Add music takes a folder or file path, or opens the Windows file picker.
  • Shuffle and repeat are next to the play button. Click a track in the list to play it, or its × to take it out of the playlist (the file stays). Clear empties the playlist.
  • Turn the music down while I talk lowers it whenever your mic hears you.
  • Hear music plays it on your Windows default output too, at Your volume.

            Getting started

            1. Install a virtual audio cable, such as the free VB-Audio Virtual Cable, and restart your PC.
            2. In Output, send the mix to the cable's playback side (CABLE Input). It's picked automatically the first time if the cable is installed.
            3. Your default microphone is added for you. Use Add source for apps (e.g. a browser or music player) or other inputs, Add sounds for the soundboard, and Add music for a playlist.
            4. In the app you talk in, choose the cable's recording side (CABLE Output) as the microphone. See Using the mic in other apps.

            Setup check in the top bar checks all of this and offers fixes.

            Using the mic in other apps

            Any app that records audio can use the mix: choose CABLE Output (VB-Audio Virtual Cable) as its microphone or input device. Keep your Windows default output on your speakers or headphones, not the cable.

            Voice chat apps often clean up your mic themselves, which can cut out or distort the soundboard and music. The mixer can do this for your mic alone instead (see Mic processing), so turn the app's versions off.

            Discord

            In Settings → Voice & Video:

            • Input Device: CABLE Output (VB-Audio Virtual Cable).
            • Input Sensitivity: turn off Automatically determine input sensitivity and drag the slider all the way left. Its automatic gate listens for speech, so it cuts out sounds and music while you aren't talking.
            • Noise Suppression: None.
            • Echo Cancellation and Automatic Gain Control: off.

            OBS

            Add an Audio Input Capture source and choose CABLE Output. If OBS also captures your mic directly, remove that source so your voice isn't recorded twice.

            Teams, Zoom and others

            Choose CABLE Output as the microphone in the app's audio settings, and turn off its noise suppression if it cuts out the soundboard.

            Sources and the mixer

            • Apps capture everything an app plays, including its child processes (all of a browser's tabs). The app keeps playing to your speakers too. Apps are matched by name, so capture resumes when the app restarts. Windows can only capture whole apps, not single windows or tabs.
            • Microphones and inputs: pick a device, or Default microphone, which follows your Windows default. Capturing the cable's own recording side would loop the mix into itself, so that's refused.
            • Master sets the level of the whole mix. A limiter stops the sum from clipping harshly.
            • Listen plays the final mix on your Windows default output (use headphones) to check what others hear. It's shared by every open mixer page and turns off when the last page that turned it on closes.

            Mic processing

            Open it with the ⚙ on a microphone's card. It only affects that mic, never apps or the soundboard.

            • Noise suppression uses a small neural network (RNNoise) to remove steady background noise such as fans, hum and keyboard clatter.
            • The noise gate silences your mic while you aren't talking. Automatic opens when it hears speech that's louder than the room's background noise. Above a level I set opens when the meter passes your line: set it just above where the meter sits while you're quiet. Either way it stays open for 200 ms after you stop, so word endings aren't cut.
            • Extra strength (automatic only) makes the gate stricter, so sudden sounds such as a glass put down or a finger snap don't open it. To do that without cutting off the start of your words it listens 30 ms ahead, which adds 30 ms delay to your mic. Keep everything else in time adds the same 30 ms delay to every other source, so music in the mix stays in time with your voice (for singing along, say).

            Soundboard

            • Add MP3, WAV, OGG, FLAC or M4A files (up to 60 seconds and 20 MB each, 64 sounds) with Add sounds or by dropping them on the soundboard.
            • Click a sound to play it; clicking it again restarts it. Stop all stops everything. A sound's ⚙ renames it, sets its own volume or deletes it.
            • Drag sounds to reorder them, or focus one and press Alt+← or Alt+→.
            • To trim a sound, open its ⚙ and drag the edges of its waveform, or type times. Trim silence cuts quiet bits off both ends, fades smooth the start and end, and Preview plays it only to you (through Hear sounds). The file isn't changed, so Reset restores it.
            • The soundboard is also a card in the mixer, with its own volume, mute and hotkeys. If you remove that card, the soundboard offers to add it back.
            • Hear sounds plays the soundboard on your Windows default output at Your volume, so you hear your sounds without hearing your own voice. It pauses while Listen is on, as Listen already includes them.

            Music

            • Add music adds folders (searched, including subfolders) or single files by their path. Copy a folder's path from Explorer's address bar, or use Folders… / Files… to pick them in a Windows file picker. Files are played where they are, not copied.
            • Tracks play one after another, from the disk, so long tracks and big folders are fine. Click a track to play it. Its × takes it out of the playlist without deleting the file; Restore removed tracks in Add music brings them back, and removing a folder forgets the tracks removed from it. Clear empties the whole playlist; again, no files are deleted. Shuffle mixes the order up; repeat goes round the list, or repeats one track.
            • Turn the music down while I talk lowers it while your mic hears you: by the noise gate if it's on, otherwise when you're loud enough.
            • The music is a card in the mixer with its own volume, mute and hotkeys (play/pause, next, previous). Hear music plays it on your Windows default output at Your volume; it pauses while Listen is on.

            Hotkeys

            • Hotkeys work while other apps are focused, such as a game, and don't block the key from reaching that app.
            • Windows hides keys typed into an app running as administrator from apps that aren't, so hotkeys stop working while such an app is focused (some games and their launchers run this way, and voice chat apps' hotkeys have the same limit). To fix it, turn on Start with Windows → as Administrator (Windows asks for permission once, then the mixer starts elevated at every sign-in without asking; it runs a copy in Program Files, which Setup check offers to update after you open a newer version), or right-click virtual-audio.exe → Run as administrator. Some anti-cheat software blocks other apps' hotkeys regardless.
            • Actions: toggle mute, push to talk (silent unless held), push to mute (silent while held), play sound, stop all sounds, and music play/pause, next and previous.
            • Click a hotkey's keys to record new ones. Keyboard combinations and the mouse side buttons (Mouse4, Mouse5) both work.

            Troubleshooting

            • Others hear nothing: check Setup check, that the app you talk in uses CABLE Output, and that the mic card's meter moves when you speak.
            • Everything I play ends up in the mic, or I hear nothing: your Windows default output is probably the cable. Set it back to your speakers or headphones in Settings → System → Sound.
            • Listen or Hear sounds is refused: they play on the Windows default output, which mustn't be the device the mix goes to.
            • Hotkeys don't work in a game: the game probably runs as administrator. See Hotkeys.
            • The gate cuts out quiet words: switch it to Above a level I set and lower the line.

            The app keeps its files in %APPDATA%\VirtualAudioMixer: config.json (your settings), sounds\, and log.txt (this run) and log.previous.txt (the run before), which the tray menu's Open log file opens. A config.json that can't be read is moved aside as config.<time>.json.bad.
